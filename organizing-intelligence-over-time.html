Organizing Intelligence Over Time

Human–AI Collaboration as Joint Cognitive Development

Zhiyu Yang · University of Texas at Dallas

Literature map ↗ GitHub ↗ Preprint forthcoming

Core position

How humans and AI work together today changes what each can do tomorrow.

Human–AI collaboration is usually evaluated one task at a time. But repeated work changes the participants themselves. The way cognitive work is divided determines who practices, who receives feedback, which trajectories are retained, and which forms of reasoning become reusable. Those experiences change later human skill, machine behavior, and coordination.

Joint cognitive development

A longitudinal loop, not a fixed team.

The survey connects within-task cognitive organization to the experience each participant accumulates, and then to the capabilities that return on the next task.

The output of one task becomes part of the cognitive state that organizes the next.

Survey structure

Three questions organize the literature.

Human cognition, Human–Automation and HCI, and modern AI agents each illuminate a different part of the same longitudinal problem.

QUESTION 1

How is cognitive work organized within a task?

Which cognitive operations are invoked, who performs them, where authority lies, and how much effort should be spent?

QUESTION 2

How does experience change the cognition available next time?

Experience can become chunks, procedures, memories, skills, routing preferences, and stopping policies that reshape later cognition.

QUESTION 3

How does organizing work change the learners themselves?

Automation and delegation determine who gets to practice, who receives feedback, and what each side can learn from.

Why now

Agent runtimes make cognitive organization executable.

Modern systems expose many choices that earlier literatures studied separately, while persistent histories make their long-run consequences increasingly observable.

Persistent experience

Memories, trajectories, workflows, skills, and other artifacts can survive beyond one interaction.

Explicit cognitive control

Reasoning, retrieval, verification, routing, escalation, and stopping can be configured as runtime decisions.

Adaptive machine behavior

Experience can change not only task content but also how future computation is selected and organized.

Changing human participation

As AI autonomy grows, people receive a different distribution of practice, oversight, and intervention opportunities.
